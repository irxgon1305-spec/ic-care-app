Understanding IC

What it is, why flares happen, your cycle & your body — gently.

🌼 What IC is

Interstitial cystitis (IC) is a chronic bladder condition — not an infection — where the bladder wall becomes irritated and inflamed. It brings pelvic pain, a frequent urge to pee, and sometimes burning. It's sometimes called "painful bladder syndrome." It's very real, but very manageable with the right habits — and it looks a little different for everyone.

🌊 Why flares happen

A flare is when symptoms spike for a few hours or days, usually set off by a trigger. Common ones:

  • Certain foods & drinks (coffee, alcohol, citrus, tomatoes, spicy food)
  • Hormonal shifts around your period
  • Stress and poor sleep
  • Holding urine too long

Triggers are common, not universal — test slowly and trust what your body tells you.

🌙 Around your period

Flaring right before or during your period is very common — not in your head. The estrogen dip plus inflammatory prostaglandins make the bladder and pelvic nerves more sensitive. Since it's predictable, go extra gentle on trigger foods 5–7 days before, lean on heat, and rest more.

🫧 What you're feeling

  • Urge with little output: an inflamed bladder over-fires "full" signals, so you go and little comes out. Not you being dramatic.
  • Burning drips: concentrated urine is more acidic, and it stings already-sensitive tissue — it can feel just like an infection even when it isn't.
  • Why gulping water can hurt: big amounts fill the bladder fast and add pressure. Small, steady sips are kinder.
📓 Tiny habit that helps: jot down what you eat and how you feel a few hours later. Over time you'll build your own personal map of what your bladder loves and what it doesn't.

⚠️ When it's not just a flare

Get same-day medical care if any of these show up:

  • Kidney or flank pain (back or side, above the waist). This is not a typical IC symptom.
  • Fever or chills
  • Nausea or vomiting along with urinary pain
  • Visible blood in urine
  • Pain that's clearly worse or different from usual flares

Tip: AZO turns urine orange and can throw off urine tests. If a UTI is possible, give a urine sample before taking AZO when you can.

Gentle Daily Habits

Small, kind things that can make each day a little easier.

You don't have to do all of these — even one on a hard day counts. Think of it as a menu of comforts, not a checklist. 💗

💊 Take Prelief before meals

It neutralizes most of the food acid before it reaches your bladder, giving you a little more freedom at the table.

💧 Sip water steadily all day

Steady sips keep your pee less concentrated and gentler on the bladder. Don't gulp large amounts at once — slow and steady is kinder.

🍽️ Eat smaller meals

A very full stomach adds pressure around the bladder. Smaller, more frequent meals tend to sit easier.

🔥 Use heat

A heating pad on your lower belly is one of the best comfort tools there is — simple, cozy, and surprisingly effective.

🧘 Rest and de-stress

Stress and poor sleep both lower your bladder's threshold, so calm and good rest genuinely help. Slowing down is part of feeling well.

📓 Track what you eat & how you feel

Your personal pattern matters most. A quick daily jot builds your own trigger map over time — no perfection needed, just gentle curiosity.

🌙 Go extra gentle before your period

The week before your period is a predictable tender window — ease off trigger foods and lean on your comforts ahead of time.

🤍 On a flare day, the only habit that matters is being soft with yourself. Water, warmth, rest, and a safe snack — that's more than enough.

Supplement Stack

Kept general — always check with a doctor or pharmacist.

🌵 Desert Harvest Low-Acid Multi-Vitamin (without B6)

  • Made for people with IC and acid sensitivity.
  • Has no vitamin B6, and uses buffered vitamin C (calcium ascorbate) instead of plain ascorbic acid.
  • Check the label: it may already contain calcium glycerophosphate. If so, ask a pharmacist whether the total dose is right with the standalone one.

🧂 Calcium Glycerophosphate

  • Taken with meals and drinks to lower their acidity.
  • A good habit before something that might be a trigger.

💧 D-Mannose occasional

  • Meant to help prevent UTIs, not IC. It doesn't calm bladder-wall irritation.
  • Research is mixed: some studies found fewer repeat UTIs, and a larger 2024 trial found no benefit.
  • It does not treat an active UTI. It's never a reason to skip getting checked.

🗓️ Birth Control

  • Part of the daily routine — take it at the same time each day so it's easy to remember.
  • Keep it on the list you show anyone who prescribes something new.

📋 Good to know

  • Calcium and antibiotics: calcium can block some antibiotics used for UTIs, such as cipro and doxycycline. If prescribed one, take it a few hours apart from calcium products and the multivitamin, and tell the pharmacist about the supplements.
  • Show this list to anyone who prescribes something new.
  • Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before starting or stopping anything.
